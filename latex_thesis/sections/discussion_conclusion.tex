\section{Discussion \& Conclusion}

% This thesis addressed the problem of meaningful artefact-level evaluative LLM measurement. Formalizing the evaluation procedure through the formative psychometric measurement model enabled us to define a theoretically substantiated relationship between criterion measurements and the target construct, while highlighting the main validity risk: construct under- or misrepresentation. To empirically test this, we designed an experimental procedure which defines criteria that constitute an increasingly rich specification of the target construct. We employ this procedure in two complementary validation settings: convergent validity through alignment with human ratings, and predictive validity through the association with a behavioral target. Diagnostic analysis of the LLM criterion measurements provide us with insights on the conditions under which the estimated relationship with the target construct is interpretable in a regression-based formative model. Together, these two research questions provide insight into the utility of LLM evaluations in operational settings, where meaningful measurement facilitates content quality assurance through automated revision, and increased understanding of text quality.  


This thesis began by defining the central measurement problem: under what conditions does LLM-based scoring become meaningful measurement? We argued that many current methods fall short of permitting construct-level claims based on criterion-level scores because of a theoretical underspecification of the measurement model. To address this problem, we consider evaluation through a psychometric lens, and define artefact-level evaluation as a formative measurement problem. In a formative model, the criteria, and hence their measurements, define the construct. This motivates the two research questions: when combined, do LLM-derived evaluations provide increased validity evidence under increasingly rich construct specifications, and are they diagnostically interpretable?
