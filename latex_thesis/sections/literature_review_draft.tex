Metrics are useful, but they are not measurement.


\subsection{From Similarity Metrics to Multidimensional Evaluation}

Early methods for automated text evaluation were dominated by similarity-based metrics. Lexical overlap metrics such as BLEU \citep{papineni_bleu_2001} and ROUGE \citep{lin_rouge_2004} compare candidate outputs to human references through shared words or $n$-grams, while later embedding-based metrics such as BERTScore \citep{zhang_bertscore_2020}, BLEURT \citep{sellam_bleurt_2020}, and MoverScore \citep{zhao_moverscore_2019} replace surface overlap with contextual semantic similarity. These methods made automated evaluation scalable, but they remain limited by their reliance on reference similarity as a proxy for output quality.
